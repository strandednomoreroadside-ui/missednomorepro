## 1. Why LinkedIn at all (the honest positioning)

Home-service **owner-operators** live on Facebook, not LinkedIn. We are not going to pretend a solo plumber is scrolling LinkedIn on a Tuesday. So LinkedIn is **not** our primary volume channel — Facebook is. LinkedIn does four specific jobs that Facebook can't do as well:

1. **Reaches the buyers who ARE on LinkedIn:** multi-location operators, franchise owners and franchisees, regional service companies, and GMs of 5–15-truck shops. These are bigger deals (more numbers = more minutes = higher plans) and they research vendors on LinkedIn before they buy.
2. **Reaches B2B referral partners** who each touch dozens of owners: distributors and suppliers (plumbing/HVAC/electrical wholesale), trade associations, franchise development teams, service-business coaches and consultants, bookkeepers/CPAs who serve the trades, and field-service software resellers. One warm partner can send us 20 owners.
3. **Builds founder credibility** — a real trades owner who built the tool for his own shop. That story is the trust asset the Facebook ads and outbound DMs borrow from. When a skeptical owner Googles us, the founder's LinkedIn should close the loop.
4. **Feeds the Outbound lane.** Every relevant comment, connection, and profile view is a warm signal the Outbound lane can act on. LinkedIn is a lead-surfacing machine; posting is what makes the surfacing happen.

**Success on LinkedIn = qualified conversations with operators/partners + credibility that lifts every other channel.** It is not measured in likes.

## 3. Content pillars (4 pillars + a rotating 5th)

Each pillar sits where our expertise, the audience's pain, and what nobody else is saying clearly all overlap. Rotate them so the feed never feels like one long ad.

**Pillar 1 — Missed-Call Math (the money pillar).**
The core idea made undeniable: a missed call is lost revenue, and here's the arithmetic. Simple, screenshot-able, save-able.
*Strategic reason:* this is our one big idea (foundation §4). It's the most shareable and the most self-qualifying — anyone who nods at the math is a prospect. Drives saves, which the algorithm weights heavily.

**Pillar 2 — Hear It For Yourself (the demo pillar).**
Content whose entire job is to get someone to call (440) 644-2423 or start the trial and let the product sell itself.
*Strategic reason:* our unfair advantage is that the product demos itself with zero founder time (foundation §6). For a receptionist, "just call it" beats any pitch. Every one of these posts is a self-running sales rep.

**Pillar 3 — Build-in-Public / Founder Story (the trust pillar).**
The honest story of a trades owner who built this for his own shop, plus what we're learning shipping it. Wins, mistakes, real decisions.
*Strategic reason:* trust is the whole game for a non-technical, corporate-averse buyer. A real owner who lived the problem is our biggest differentiator vs. faceless SaaS. This is also the pillar that earns follows and inbound DMs.

**Pillar 4 — Myth-Busting & Comparisons (the objection pillar).**
Kill the objections in public: "AI will sound like a robot," "my customers will hate it," "it'll make up prices," "an answering service is fine." Us vs. voicemail, vs. answering services, vs. cheap bots.
*Strategic reason:* the sale is lost or won on objections. Handling them in content means the trial starts already de-risked, and it gives us a defensible take (foundation §5) that neutral vendors won't touch.

**Pillar 5 (rotating) — Operator & Partner Angle (the LinkedIn-native pillar).**
Content aimed specifically at the people who are actually on LinkedIn: multi-location operators (math across N locations), franchisees, and referral partners (suppliers/associations/coaches). Includes vertical spotlights (plumbing week, HVAC week, roadside week).
*Strategic reason:* this is what makes LinkedIn worth doing at all (see §1). Bigger deals and one-to-many partner relationships live here. Use it ~1x/week.

**Mix target per week:** Pillar 1 ×1–2, Pillar 2 ×1, Pillar 3 ×1, Pillar 4 ×1, Pillar 5 ×1. Roughly 40% money/demo, 30% trust, 30% objection/operator.

**Rules that apply to every post (LinkedIn algorithm hygiene):**
- Hook in the **first line** — it has to earn the "…see more" click before anyone sees the rest.
- **No external links in the post body.** Put the trial link in the **first comment** and say "link in comments." (LinkedIn suppresses posts with outbound links in the body.) The demo *phone number* is fine in the body — it's not a link.
- **3–5 specific hashtags**, not generic ones. Rotate from: `#homeservices #tradesbusiness #plumbing #HVAC #smallbusinessowner #missedcalls #fieldservice #contractorlife #roofing #localbusiness`. Pick the 3–5 that match the post.
- One idea per paragraph, 1–3 lines each. White space is engagement.
- End with a question or a call — invite a reply, don't broadcast.
- **Never post and ghost:** be around for ~30–45 min after each post to answer comments (see §8). If you scheduled it, set a phone reminder.

## 7. Low-touch engagement & growth tactics (the part that expands reach WITHOUT daily labor)

The posts are the fuel; this is the engine — and it's built to run on ~30 minutes, 2–3x a week, not daily.

### 7.1 Comment strategy (30 min, 2x/week — highest ROI activity on LinkedIn)
- **Target list of ~15–20 accounts** to comment on: trade associations, franchise networks, field-service software voices, home-service business coaches, and any multi-location operators who post. Build the list once; reuse it.
- **Leave 5–8 substantive comments per session** — a real extension of their point, not "great post!" One genuine sentence that adds a thought. This puts you in front of THEIR audience (which is full of your ICP) with zero content creation.
- **Comment ~30 min BEFORE you publish your own post.** It primes the feed and warms the algorithm — your post then goes out into an active session.

### 7.2 Connection strategy (build the list once, send in batches)
- Send **10–15 personalized connection requests per week** (well under LinkedIn's ~limits, keeps you safe). Batch it into one sitting.
- Prioritize: multi-location operators, franchisees, and the referral partners from §7.1.
- **Never use the default "I'd like to add you."** One-line personal note referencing their content or their business. Example: *"Saw you run [X] locations — I build an AI receptionist for home-service shops and I'm obsessed with the missed-call math at scale. Would love to connect."*
- Acceptance from a warm, content-referencing note runs far higher than cold. Target 30%+.

### 7.3 Reposting & repurposing (near-zero effort, extends every asset)
- **Repost your own best post** ~2 weeks later with a fresh one-line take on top. New audience sees it; costs 30 seconds.
- **Repurpose across channels:** every post here is already trades-plain — hand the best 3–4 to the Facebook lane to adapt (they'll rework tone for FB groups). Write once, run twice.
- **Turn your top-performing text post into the carousel (Post 5) format** if it isn't already — same idea, more reach, more dwell time.

### 7.4 DM handoff to the Outbound lane (keep founder time minimal)
- LinkedIn posting *surfaces* warm signals; it should not turn the founder into a full-time DMer. When someone comments with real interest, likes multiple posts, or accepts a connection warmly → **hand the name to the Outbound lane** with the context (which post, what they said).
- The founder's ONLY DM job: reply to inbound DMs that are clearly hot ("how much / how do I try it"), and always point them to the two self-serve CTAs — **call (440) 644-2423** and **start the trial** — never to a mandatory sales call.
- Suggested reply template for an inbound "tell me more": *"Easiest thing — call (440) 644-2423 and talk to it yourself, takes 2 minutes. If it passes, there's a 7-day free trial at missednomorepro.com and it'll call you answering as your own business. Happy to answer anything after you've heard it."*

### 7.5 What NOT to do (protects founder time & reach)
- Don't post daily. 4x/week beats 7x/week when 7 means burnout and thin posts.
- Don't put links in the post body (kills reach). First comment only.
- Don't tag people/companies unless genuinely relevant — tag-spam suppresses reach.
- Don't chase likes. Track comments, saves, profile views, connection accepts, and DMs — those predict pipeline.
